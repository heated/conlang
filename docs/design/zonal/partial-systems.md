# Partial systems: zonal/global products that are not full languages

Workshop survey (Edward, 2026-08-14: "explore a global or romance
zonal that's not a full language — whats the competition, NATO
phonetic? reduced english?"). Companion to Track E
(english-plus-channels.md); this generalizes it: the sub-language
space is where most historically *successful* constructed systems
live. See also the 2026-08-22 trailhead
(../partial-auxlang-landscape.md): the full adoption-tiered
inventory, the machine-mediation/institutional-mandate forces, and
the four competition shapes (revision inside institutions,
unclaimed channels, riding defaults, density niches).

## 1. Incumbent survey

- **NATO/ICAO spelling alphabet** (standardized 1956): 26 words;
  the most-adopted constructed convention in history. Won via
  aviation/military mandate, then cultural osmosis into customer
  service and daily life. Letters only.
- **Aviation number pronunciation** ("niner, tree, fife"):
  confusability patches on English digits — solves robustness, not
  density; no checksum; mandate-driven.
- **ICAO phraseology / IMO SMCP** (2001): fixed-phrase protocols for
  radio; safety domains absorb the training cost because regulation
  demands it.
- **ASD-STE100**: controlled documentation register; survives only
  under industry mandate (the incentive-mismatch diagnosis from the
  AZ-a discussion).
- **Reduced Englishes**: Ogden's Basic English (850 words, 1930 —
  died), Nerrière's Globish (1500, 2004 — modest), **VOA
  Special/Learning English (1500 words, since 1959 — the quiet
  winner: 65+ years of continuous use)**. Lesson: reduced English
  works as a *broadcast/production register* maintained by an
  institution, not as a thing individuals study.
- **Written conventions that won globally**: ISO 8601 dates, SI
  units, ISO currency codes, country phone codes. Pure conventions +
  institutional push. Existence proof for mode-like adoption.
- **Mediterranean Lingua Franca** (~15th–19th c.): the actual
  historical "Romance zonal that isn't a full language" — a
  Romance-lexicon trade pidgin with minimal grammar that served
  ports for centuries. Died of schooling and nation-states, not of
  inadequacy.
- **Machine translation**: the modern elephant. Eats the
  full-language use case; structurally weak at (a) noise/offline,
  (b) glanceable *shared artifacts* — signage and menus can't run an
  app per reader, (c) safety-critical confirmation (no integrity
  guarantee), (d) latency in live speech.

**Adoption pattern**: partial systems win via mandated trained
domains, institutional written conventions, or maintained broadcast
registers — never via individual study decisions.

## 2. Use-case inventory for partial systems

names/spelling exchange · numbers/dates/times/prices exchange ·
transactional travel basics · cross-language signage & labeling ·
emergency/disaster communication · safety-critical confirmation
(dosage, account, coordinates) · international documentation ·
broadcast news for learners.

## 3. Our candidates (ranked)

1. **Civilian readback layer** (safety positioning REMOVED pending
   human-factors validation — 2026-08-15 review) — modes (hu/ho/hi/he) +
   checksum + a ~10-word confirmation micro-vocabulary (yes / no /
   repeat / correct / wrong / stop / over). Aviation has readback
   discipline; civilians have *nothing* for medication dosages,
   account numbers, addresses, codes. **No incumbent occupies this
   niche.** Machinery: already built (modes.md, tools/modes.py).
   Next artifact: one-page protocol card + the confirmation words
   (drawn from the greenfield particle space or kept English —
   decide by use context).
2. **RZ-lite: pan-Romance transactional register** — ~200 words
   (subset of rz-lexicon.md) + ~10 syntactic patterns (noun phrase,
   imperative, intonation question, numbers, prices), plural -s as
   the only morphology. An afternoon to learn productively;
   readable natively by ~900M. Use: signage, menus, tourism,
   relief labeling across Romance countries. The Lingua Franca's
   designed descendant. Testable with the existing cloze
   instrument (its items are already mostly RZ-lite!).
3. **"NATO for quantities" positioning** — don't compete with the
   spelling alphabet's install base; complement it: our frames beat
   the incumbents on numbers/dates/times (density, checksum, no
   date-order ambiguity vs "fife niner" patches). Pitchable to the
   same trained domains NATO already conditioned.

Deliberately not pursued: another reduced English (AZ-a toggle
covers our stake, VOA owns the register), another spelling alphabet
(NATO is unbeatable on letters).

## 4. Relationship to the portfolio

These are the zero-to-low-commitment rungs of the spectrum
(greenfield → RZ → AZ-toggle → Track E → partial systems). They
also serve as *adoption wedges for the bigger artifacts*: someone
using the readback layer has learned the digit code, which is 40% of
the greenfield's mode system; someone reading RZ-lite signage is
pre-trained for RZ proper.
