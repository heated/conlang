Status: **READY TO LAUNCH** 2026-08-30. Design: `cloze-test-v0.md`
§v1. Budget ceiling $30 (Edward).

The Interlingua renderings are the agent's. Every **visible** word in
them has now been verified as attested in running Interlingua prose on
ia.wikipedia (log at the end) — no community contact required, per
Edward's 2026-08-30 direction to land quickly with minimal engagement.
The key insight that made this cheap: a *blanked* word is deleted from
the passage, so only the words a participant actually reads need to be

`empleos`) and the passages were rewritten to use attested ones

source anywhere (conlang-7ds).

## 1. What the participant sees (Google Form, one page per passage)

**Intro (English, then repeated in ES/PT/IT/FR by the launcher if
desired):**

> You will read two short passages in a language you have probably
> never seen. It is designed to be readable by people who read
> Spanish, Portuguese, Italian or French. Some words are missing.
> **Type what you think each missing word means — in your own
> language or in English.** Guessing is fine; if you truly have no
> idea, leave it blank. There are no right answers about you; we are
> testing the language, not you. About 4 minutes.
>
> Do not look anything up. Do not use translation tools.

**Consent line (required checkbox):**

> I am 18 or older. I understand my answers will be used, without my
> name or Prolific ID, in a public research write-up about a
> constructed language, and that I can leave at any time (my
> submission is then discarded). I confirm I have not used a
> dictionary or translation tool.

**Group assignment.** Four form variants (A-RZ then D-IA; D-IA then
A-RZ; A-IA then D-RZ; D-RZ then A-IA). Prolific distributes
participants across four studies of ~6–7 each, or one study with a
randomizer link. Each participant sees exactly one RZ passage and
one Interlingua passage, never the same text twice.

**Per passage:** the passage with numbered blanks (10 short-answer
fields), then one gist item: *"In one sentence, what was this text
about?"* (free text, scored pass/fail).

**Exit questions:** (1) first language (single choice: ES / PT / IT
/ FR / other); (2) other Romance languages you read comfortably
(multi); (3) *"Have you ever studied Interlingua, Esperanto, or
another constructed language?"* (yes / no — yes is excluded from the
primary analysis and reported separately); (4) Prolific completion
code shown on submit.

Key: 26 hodie/today · 27 solar · 28 tres/three · 29 pais/country ·
30 investimento/investment · 31 crear~creara/create (the Interlingua
slot takes the synthetic future *creara*; any answer meaning
"create/will create" scores) · 32 reducer~reducera/reduce · 33 cento/
percent · 34 anuncio~annuncio/announcement · 35 transparencia~
transparentia/transparency.

Note on the deltas exercised: A contrasts orthography (`norte/nord`,
`viajator/viagiator`, `manto/mantello`, `soplar/sufflar`,
`brilar/brillar`, `reconocer/recognoscer`), the copula (`era/esseva`),
plural article + agreement (`les dos/le duo`), and the Ibero pronoun
(`el/ille`). D contrasts `governo/governamento`, `-cion/-tion`,
`en/in`, `segun/secundo`, `per cento/pro cento`, `sobre/super`,
analytic vs synthetic future (`va crear/creara`), adjective number
agreement (`ambientales/ambiental`), and natural gender on the animate
noun (`ministra/ministro` — RZ marks it, Interlingua does not here).
All of those are visible, non-blank context words; the blanks
themselves test the same ten meanings in both renderings. Those are exactly the surface
deltas the profile decision lists.

## 3. Scoring

Semantic match against the key, by hand, blind to condition (the
scorer sees the answer and the slot key, not which passage variant
the participant saw). Accept any language. Accept synonyms that fit
the slot (*cape* for *manto*; *admit / acknowledge / recognise* for
*reconocer*). Reject wrong-meaning near-cognates (*remover* → "to
move" is wrong; "remove / take off" is right). Gist: pass if the
sentence identifies the situation (wind and sun compete; a government
energy programme).

Two published numbers per passage and pooled: **specifics** (cloze %
correct) and **gist** (pass rate). Report per first language.

## 4. Analysis (precommitted)

- Primary: mean cloze % on the **RZ** passages, pooled and per L1,
  with a 95% CI (Wilson on item-level, and a participant-level
  bootstrap; report both). Same for Interlingua passages. Gist rates
  alongside.
- Secondary, exploratory: paired difference (RZ − Interlingua) per
  participant, bootstrap CI. **Stated in advance: n≈25 detects a
  paired effect of roughly 15 points on a 10-item passage; a small
  real delta will read as a null and must be reported as
  "inconclusive," never as "no difference."**
- Exclusions, decided before unblinding: self-reported conlang
  study; median completion under 90 s; all blanks empty.
- Per-item failure rates, for the lexicon: an item most participants
  miss is a design bug; an item one L1 misses is a weighting bug.

Script: **`tools/cloze_analysis.py`** — written and self-tested. It
reads the form's CSV export, applies the exclusions BEFORE computing
any outcome, scores by meaning in any language, and prints the tables
above. The precommitments are enforced in code rather than left to
discipline: the script prints the minimum detectable effect beside the
observed difference, and when the CI spans zero it prints
**INCONCLUSIVE** with the explicit instruction not to report a null.
Run `python3 tools/cloze_analysis.py --selftest` to see it work on
synthetic data.

`annuncio` and `transparentia` were also not found — and do not matter:

**Residual risk, stated honestly.** Attestation on Wikipedia shows a
form is used by Interlingua writers; it does not prove it is the
Interlingua–English Dictionary's preferred form, and the *style* of
these passages has had no native-equivalent review. For a study whose
Interlingua arm is explicitly exploratory and underpowered, that is a
proportionate standard. If the arm is ever promoted to a headline
comparison, it needs a real Interlingua reader first.
