# Efficiency comparison v0: greenfield vs RZ vs English
(workshop draft — the tradeoff-clarity deliverable, Edward's lane 2)

First like-for-like measurement, on the fable's opening two sentences.
All three versions carry the same propositional content. Greenfield
uses gf-grammar.md + gf-seed-lexicon.md (seed-stage; romanization
with computed check doubling omitted here for legibility — lengths
counted on the plain segmental forms).

## The texts

**English** (26 words, 33 syllables):
> The north wind and the sun disputed over which of them was the
> stronger, when a traveler came along wrapped in a warm cloak.

**RZ** (22 words, 40 syllables):
> Le vento del norte e le sol disputava sobre qui era le plus forte,
> quando un viajator passava, coprite de un manto calde.

**Greenfield** (17 words, 30 syllables):
> weto nos ha so hoon luta, cu hees la mus pus; wajo-ne hoon ne,
> kopa-s his manto kalo-s.
>
> Gloss: wind north-MOD and sun PAST dispute, which of them more-MOD
> strong-MOD; journey-person PAST go, covered-MOD with cloak warm-MOD.
> (Seed-stage syntax; "wajo-ne" = journey-goer compound pending the
> compounding rule; hyphens are gloss aids, not orthography.)

## Honest observations

1. **The greenfield's compression is real but modest in speech**
   (30 vs 33 syllables vs English) — its wins concentrate in the
   WRITTEN and MOTOR projections: 17 characters vs ~115 Latin chars,
   17 projected strokes vs ~40. The thesis was always that text and
   typing are where the architecture pays; this first measurement
   agrees.
2. **RZ is the least dense spoken form** (40 syllables — Romance
   syllable inflation) and buys its keep entirely through the
   zero-learning column. Each language wins exactly one column
   decisively: English (installed base), RZ (receptive access),
   greenfield (written/motor density).
3. Caveats: n=2 sentences; greenfield translation is seed-stage (no
   validated compounding rule; particle senses untested); stroke
   counts are projections (no chord layout exists — flagged repeatedly
   in reviews); English char count includes spaces.
4. Next measurements that would matter: a 100-sentence parallel
   corpus once the greenfield lexicon reaches ~200 roots; reading-
   size-adjusted page area (fused chars need larger bodies than Latin
   — the fusion study's H-dependence prices this); actual chord
   layouts before any stroke claim leaves this table.
