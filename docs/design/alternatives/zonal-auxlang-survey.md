# Zonal Auxiliary Languages: A Survey

Research note, 2026-08-09. Web survey to ground the choice of zone for a
chorded-input layer on top of a zonal auxlang (Romance-first plan). Factual and
cited; strategic implications limited to the final section.

A *zonal* auxiliary language targets speakers of one language family (Slavic,
Romance, Germanic, ...) rather than the whole world. The design center of
gravity is therefore *familiarity and immediate passive understanding* rather
than the simplicity/regularity that global IALs optimize for — Merunka & van
Steenbergen make this contrast explicitly ([Merunka et al. 2019, IJEG][ijeg];
[Wikipedia: Zonal auxiliary language][wp-zonal]).

---

## 1. Romance zone

### 1.1 Interlingua (IALA, 1951)

- **Design**: naturalistic. Vocabulary is extracted by a prototyping method:
  a word enters if present in at least three of four control language groups
  (English, French, Italian, Spanish/Portuguese as one unit), with German and
  Russian as secondary controls. Grammar is stripped-Romance (no
  noun–adjective agreement, no personal verb inflection). Developed by the
  International Auxiliary Language Association (IALA) 1937–1951; IALA closed
  in 1953, so the language has been essentially frozen since publication
  ([Wikipedia: Interlingua][wp-ia]; [Wikipedia: IALA][wp-iala]).
- **Receptive intelligibility**: the core claim is *readability without
  study* for Romance speakers and educated English speakers. Historical
  evidence is real but informal: in the 1950s–60s roughly 30 scientific
  (mainly medical) journals published article summaries in Interlingua, and
  medical congresses distributed Interlingua summaries; Swedish school
  experiments reported that pupils taught Interlingua for a year could read
  Spanish, Italian and Portuguese newspaper text "at sight" with good
  comprehension ([Wikipedia: Interlingua][wp-ia]; [Omniglot:
  Interlingua][omni-ia]). No controlled, published comprehension percentages
  were located (contrast Interslavic, §4).
- **Community**: no native speakers; estimates run from "a few hundred"
  active users (2007 estimate cited on Wikipedia) up to ~1,500, with
  concentrations in Scandinavia ([Wikipedia: Interlingua][wp-ia];
  [Grokipedia: Interlingua][grok-ia]). By conlang standards the *corpus and
  materials* base is large: the Interlingua–English Dictionary (27k words),
  the Gode & Blair grammar, decades of periodicals.
- **Corpus / infrastructure**: Union Mundial pro Interlingua (UMI), founded
  1955 at the first Interlingua congress in Tours, runs biennial international
  conferences (23rd: Prague 2019; 26th: Bad Marienberg) and publishes the
  bimonthly magazine *Panorama in Interlingua* (since 1988) ([Wikipedia:
  UMI][wp-umi]; [Wikipedia: Panorama][wp-pan]; [UIA profile][uia-umi]).
  Interlingua also has a Wikipedia edition and active Facebook groups.
- **Orthography**: 26-letter Latin alphabet, **no diacritics**, largely
  phonemic but etymologizing: double consonants preserved, soft/hard c and g,
  Greek digraphs (ph, th, ch) and y in Hellenisms. An official simplified
  "collateral orthography" exists but is little used ([Wikipedia:
  Interlingua][wp-ia]).
- **Governance**: no active language academy; UMI is promotional, not
  regulatory. The 1951 standard is stable by inertia.

### 1.2 Romance Neolatino / Vía Neolatina (2006–present)

- **Design**: naturalistic *pan-Romance standard* rather than a simplified
  IAL: full Romance grammar (gender/number agreement, personal verb
  inflection). Codified "scientifically" by the Vía Neolatina collective of
  Romance linguists led by Dr. Jordi Cassany-Bates (started 2006), using an
  explicit methodology with 20+ selection criteria (intercomprehension,
  regularity, tradition, conciseness...) ([Wikipedia: Pan-Romance
  language][wp-panrom]; [neolatino.eu][neolatino]).
- **Publications**: *Grammatica Essentiale Neolatina* and *Dictionario
  Essentiale Neolatino* (April 2019); online dictionary and conjugator
  ([neolatino.eu dictionary PDF][neolatino-dict]; [Omniglot:
  Neolatin][omni-neo]).
- **Intelligibility**: designed for it; no measured studies located. The
  Pan-Romance Wikipedia article's framing: pan-Romance varieties are
  "potentially understandable by an audience of up to 800 million" Romance
  speakers, but this is a potential-audience figure, not a test result
  ([Wikipedia: Pan-Romance language][wp-panrom]).
- **Community**: small but active and multi-platform — Facebook groups,
  Discord, Telegram (incl. the cross-project group *LINGUA(S)
  PAN-ROMANICA(S)*), a subreddit, Google Groups, blogs ([Omniglot:
  Neolatin][omni-neo]; [neolatino.eu][neolatino]). No credible size estimate
  found; visible activity suggests dozens-to-low-hundreds of participants,
  i.e., orders of magnitude below Interslavic.
- **Orthography**: etymological pan-Romance spelling with accent marks
  (following Romance traditions); more diacritics and less
  grapheme–phoneme regularity than Interlingua.
- **Governance**: single lead developer + collaborating linguists; coherent
  but founder-centric.

### 1.3 Latino sine flexione (Peano, 1903)

- **Design**: Latin vocabulary with inflection deleted (schematic
  simplification of a classical language, not a synthesis of modern Romance).
  Promoted through Peano's Academia pro Interlingua (founded 1908)
  ([Wikipedia: LsF][wp-lsf]).
- **Status**: effectively **dormant**. It declined after Peano's death
  (1932); the Academia did not survive WWII; contemporary activity is
  hobbyist curiosity only. Even in its prime, "most mathematicians were put
  off by the artificial appearance of the language"
  ([Wikipedia: LsF][wp-lsf]; [HandWiki: LsF][hw-lsf]).

### 1.4 Newer / smaller Romance projects

- **Latino Interromanico** (Raymund Zacharias, 2017; with Thiago Sanctus):
  etymological approach — words traced to common (mostly Latin) roots with
  minimal adjustment. Grammar published as *Prima/Secunda/Tertia Revista*
  (2019–2021). Community ~45 WhatsApp members as of Oct 2019 plus a Facebook
  group ([Wikipedia: Pan-Romance language][wp-panrom];
  [interromanico.auxlang.eu][interrom]).
- **Interlingua Romanica** (2001): re-Romancified Interlingua (restored
  agreement and verb inflection); abandoned by its authors in 2008; residual
  Facebook community (~175 members, 2019) ([Wikipedia: Pan-Romance
  language][wp-panrom]).
- **Historical line**: Stefan George's poetic *Lingua Romana* (1889); Ørberg's
  Neolatino (1941, abandoned); André Schild's *Neolatino* (1947 — briefly had
  a bulletin and community, faded by 1949); Campos Lima's *Internacional*
  (1948); Zoltán Magyar's *Romanid* (Hungary, 1956/1957) ([Wikipedia:
  Pan-Romance language][wp-panrom]; [Wikipedia: Romanid][wp-romanid]). A
  century of pan-Romance projects, none of which built a durable community.
- **Romance-adjacent, different design philosophy**:
  - *Lingua Franca Nova / Elefen* (Boeree, publ. 1998): Romance lexicon on a
    creole-like, fully regular grammar; **highly phonemic, diacritic-free
    orthography**. Small but persistent community (Discord most active;
    Facebook, subreddit); has its own Wikipedia edition ([Omniglot:
    LFN][omni-lfn]; [jayeless.net][jay-lfn]; [lingclusive][ling-lfn]).
  - *Occidental/Interlingue* (de Wahl, 1922): naturalistic Western-Romance
    flavored IAL; declined after Interlingua appeared (1951) but has a
    notable internet-era revival community ([Wikipedia:
    Interlingue][wp-ile]).

**Romance zone summary**: many projects, long pedigree, no winner. The zone's
total *constructed-language* community across all pan-Romance projects is in
the hundreds-to-low-thousands, with Interlingua holding most of the corpus and
institutional history and Neolatino holding most of the current design energy.

## 2. Benchmark zone: Interslavic (Medžuslovjansky)

### 2.1 History and governance

Modern Interslavic descends from *Slovianski* (Jan van Steenbergen and
others, 2006) and *Neoslavonic* (Vojtěch Merunka, 2009). In 2011 Slovianski,
Slovioski and Novoslověnsky merged under the name "Interslavic"; after the
first CISLa conference (Staré Město, 2017) the two remaining standards were
unified by a five-member committee — Merunka, van Steenbergen, Lombino, Swat,
Skrylev — and the language is now regulated by the **Interslavic Committee**
([Wikipedia: Interslavic][wp-isv]; [van Steenbergen:
introduction][steen-intro]; [HandWiki: Interslavic][hw-isv]). ISO 639-3 code
`isv` was granted in **April 2024** after failed applications in 2012, 2014
and 2019 ([Wikipedia: Interslavic][wp-isv]).

### 2.2 Community size and trajectory

| Year | Estimate | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2012 | "several hundreds" (Slovianski) | Iliev, via [Wikipedia][wp-isv] |
| 2017 | ~2,000 active users | Kocór et al. 2017, via [Wikipedia][wp-isv] |
| 2020 | ~7,000 | via [Wikipedia][wp-isv] |
| 2022 | ~20,000 (community, not active speakers) | *Mel* magazine, via [Wikipedia][wp-isv] |
| 2024 | main Facebook group ~20,800 (from ~1,900 mid-2017); Discord ~8,300; VK ~2,300; total est. 20–25k | [Grokipedia: Interslavic][grok-isv] |

Sustained platform growth (Facebook, Discord, VK), a webzine
([interslavic.news][isv-news]), a portal ([interslavic-language.org][isv-org]),
learning site with dictionary/translator ([interslavic.fun][isv-fun]), *The
Little Prince* translation (2021), and a first full-length original novel
(*Nehaj Nesut Nas Běle Oblaky*, 2025) ([Wikipedia: Interslavic][wp-isv]).

### 2.3 The film moment

*The Painted Bird* (Václav Marhoul, 2019) is the first feature film with
Interslavic dialogue (~9 minutes subtitled, plus unsubtitled scenes). Marhoul
chose it — reportedly after googling "Slavic Esperanto" — precisely because it
is recognizably Slavic while attributable to no nation, so no country would be
identified with the villagers' atrocities. Merunka coached the actors. The
film premiered in competition at Venice and generated significant press for
the language ([Radio Prague interview with Merunka][rp-merunka]; [van
Steenbergen: The Painted Bird][steen-bird]; [Wikipedia: The Painted
Bird][wp-bird]).

### 2.4 Measured intelligibility (primary source)

Merunka, Molhanec, van Steenbergen et al., *"The Interslavic language as a
tool for supporting e-democracy in Central and Eastern Europe"*, Int. J.
Electronic Governance 11(3/4), 2019 ([PDF][ijeg]) — an online cloze-test
survey run 2015–June 2018:

- **1,822 valid responses** from Slavic-language speakers (plus 56 non-native
  respondents), results re-weighted by national populations.
- Test: fill 7 missing words in a professional text in Interslavic.
- **Group means fell between 79% and 93%; overall mean 84%** (≈6 of 7 words).
  Only 18% of respondents (315/1,766) scored below 5/7.
- Education effect: university-educated 88% vs. 72–73% without university.
- Self-assessment (mean 70%) *under*-predicts measured comprehension (84%).
- Baseline asymmetries among natural languages in the same survey: Ukrainians
  understand Russian at 80%, but Russians understand Ukrainian at only 46%
  and Belarusian at 39% — i.e., a designed central standard beats the largest
  natural language as a pan-Slavic medium.

Caveats: self-selected online sample, community-run (not independently
replicated); a Leiden MA thesis (Joannot 2021) analyzes Interslavic's
intelligibility-maximizing structure comparatively but is not a comprehension
experiment ([Leiden thesis][leiden]; survey summary also in [Grokipedia:
Interslavic][grok-isv]).

### 2.5 Orthography

Deliberately **dual-script**: parallel standard Latin and Cyrillic alphabets.
Latin: 23 base letters (no q/w/x) + š, ž, č, ě + digraphs dž, lj, nj.
Cyrillic: 29 letters restricted to what national Cyrillic orthographies share
(+ є, ы, ј, љ, њ). Spelling is phonemic; optional "etymological" letters and
**flavorization** (systematic surface-tuning of output toward a specific
Slavic audience) are defined practices with tool support ([interslavic.fun:
orthography][isv-orth]; [van Steenbergen: flavorization][steen-flav]).

### 2.6 Why Interslavic has traction — concrete bootstrap mechanics

1. **Receptive-first value proposition**: one writer reaches ~300M readers
   with no learning demanded of the readers; the 84% cloze result makes the
   pitch credible and the *Painted Bird* made it vivid.
2. **Merger instead of fragmentation**: the 2011 and 2017 mergers converted
   competing dialects into one standard with a named committee — the exact
   opposite of Folkspraak's fate (§3.1).
3. **A media moment**: the 2019 film produced mainstream press (Radio Prague,
   festival coverage) and a visible recruitment spike in the community's
   growth curve (FB ~1,900 in 2017 → ~20,800 by 2024).
4. **Tooling**: interslavic.fun's multi-language dictionary, Latin↔Cyrillic
   transliterator, and flavorization support lower the cost of *producing*
   correct text — the binding constraint for a receptive-first language.
5. **Legitimacy tokens**: academic papers (IJEG, Springer chapters), CISLa
   conferences, ISO 639-3 code (2024), a translated classic and an original
   novel — each one a press hook and a credibility signal.
6. **Script/orthography familiarity**: dual-script standards plus
   flavorization meet every reader in their own writing system.

## 3. Other zones, briefly

- **Pan-Germanic — Folkspraak** (started 1995): collaborative project that
  never converged on a standard; community rifts spawned variants
  (Middelsprake, Frenkisch, ...); development "incomplete" with modest wiki
  activity (last documented edits 2023). Effectively moribund as a unified
  project ([Wikipedia: Pan-Germanic language][wp-pangerm]; [Grokipedia:
  Folkspraak][grok-folk]). Diagnosis in the literature: no single authority,
  and English already occupies the inter-Germanic niche.
- **Pan-Turkic — Ortatürk**: statistical "averaged language" (Karimov &
  Mutalov 1992, formula over 5 then 8 Turkic languages); the World Assembly
  of Turkic Peoples founded an International Institute of the Language
  Ortaturk (2008); Mireev published *Öztürk Tili* (2023, 16k+ words). Real
  institutional gestures, negligible speaker community ([HandWiki:
  Ortatürk][hw-ortaturk]). The zone's *actual* convergence energy is
  state-level script harmonization: the Organization of Turkic States agreed
  on a common 34-letter Latin alphabet in Sept 2024 ([AA news][aa-turkic]) —
  orthographic convergence of natural languages, not a zonal conlang.
- **Nordic — SamSkandinavisk**: naturalistic auxlang over Danish/Norwegian/
  Swedish; ~3,000-word lexicon, hobby-scale, incomplete ([Linguifex:
  SamSkandinavisk][lfx-sam]). Structural headwind: mainland Scandinavian
  *semicommunication* (each speaks their own language) already works, so a
  constructed intermediary adds little ([ijeg] discusses this baseline).
- **Pan-Arabic / Maghrebi**: no notable constructed zonal auxlang located;
  standard zonal-language surveys list none ([Wikipedia: Zonal auxiliary
  language][wp-zonal]). Modern Standard Arabic already functions as the
  zone's (natural, high-prestige) roofing standard, crowding out the niche.
- **East Asian (CJK)**: no genetic family — Chinese, Japanese, Korean,
  Vietnamese share Sinitic vocabulary strata, not descent — so the zonal
  recipe (average cognates) doesn't apply. Only micro hobby projects exist,
  e.g. Tonuao (~2000-character base, Latin primary script)
  ([GitHub: tonuao][gh-tonuao]). Not viable as a bootstrap zone.

## 4. Intelligibility evidence across zones

| Language | Evidence type | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Interslavic** | Online cloze test, 1,822 Slavic respondents, 2015–2018, population-weighted | Group means 79–93%; overall 84%; 82% of respondents ≥5/7 words | [Merunka et al. 2019][ijeg] |
| **Interslavic** (context) | Same survey, natural-language baselines | RU→UK 46%, RU→BE 39%, UK→RU 80% — designed standard beats any single natural Slavic language for symmetric reach | [Merunka et al. 2019][ijeg] |
| **Interlingua** | 1950s practice evidence: ~30 journals ran Interlingua abstracts; congress summaries; Swedish school "at sight" reading of Es/It/Pt after 1 yr of Interlingua | Qualitative; no published percentages found | [Wikipedia: Interlingua][wp-ia]; [Omniglot][omni-ia] |
| **Romance natural languages** | Computational intelligibility metric validated against human cloze tests (Fr/It/Pt/Es/Ro; no conlangs included) | Confirms high but *asymmetric* intra-Romance intelligibility; methodology reusable for testing a Romance zonal lang | [arXiv 2602.07447][arxiv-rom] |
| **Slavic natural languages** | Academic mutual-intelligibility research (Gooskens et al.) | Baseline for what a zonal lang must beat | [Springer: West/South Slavic][springer-slav] |
| **Neolatino, Interromanico, LFN, Folkspraak, Ortatürk** | — | No measured comprehension studies located | — |

Notable gap: **no pan-Romance conlang has modern measured intelligibility
data**. The Interslavic survey methodology (7-word cloze on a professional
text, online, self-selected) is cheap to replicate for any Romance candidate,
and the arXiv 2026 computational metric offers a zero-subject pre-screen.

## 5. Bootstrap mechanics: what made zonal langs grow or stall

Synthesis of §§1–4:

**Growth factors (all present in Interslavic, mostly absent elsewhere):**

1. **Receptive-first usability with proof.** The product works on day one for
   a writer whose audience never studies it. Interslavic paired the claim
   with numbers (84% mean cloze). Interlingua's 1950s heyday — journal
   abstracts, congress summaries — was exactly this use case, and it faded
   when English swallowed scientific publishing, not because comprehension
   failed.
2. **One standard, one named authority.** Interslavic's 2011/2017 mergers and
   standing committee ended standard-wars; Folkspraak, which never converged,
   is the control group. A founder-only authority is fragile the other way:
   Latino sine flexione died with Peano; Schild's 1947 Neolatino faded within
   two years.
3. **A media moment attached to a story.** *The Painted Bird* (2019) is the
   single clearest inflection in any zonal lang's growth curve (FB ~1,900 →
   ~20,800 between 2017 and 2024). The moment worked because infrastructure
   (site, dictionary, community) existed to catch the inbound interest.
4. **Production tooling.** Receptive languages bottleneck on *writers*, so
   dictionaries, transliterators, flavorizers, and conjugators
   (interslavic.fun; neolatino.eu's conjugator) are the growth levers.
5. **Legitimacy tokens** — ISO codes, conferences, academic papers, film
   credits, published books — compound: each unlocks press and institutional
   conversations.
6. **Meeting readers in their own script/orthography** (Interslavic
   dual-script + flavorization). Familiar surface form is part of the
   receptive bargain.

**Stall factors:**

- **The niche is already occupied**: by English (pan-Germanic, and
  post-1960s scientific Interlingua), by a natural roofing standard (MSA for
  Arabic), or by working semicommunication (Scandinavia).
- **Fragmentation** into competing standards (Folkspraak; pre-2011
  Slavic projects; the long list of mutually oblivious pan-Romance projects).
- **Founder dependence** without institutional succession (LsF, Schild,
  arguably a risk for Neolatino and Interromanico today).
- **No zone at all** (CJK — shared vocabulary without genetic unity).
- **Frozen standard + promotional-only body** (Interlingua after 1953:
  large corpus, no mechanism to evolve or campaign).

## 7. Implications for us

Judged on the three axes that matter for a chorded-input layer:

- **Receptive intelligibility.** The Slavic zone is the only one with
  measured numbers (84% mean cloze), and they also show a designed standard
  out-reaching the biggest natural language of its family. The Romance zone
  plausibly matches or beats this ceiling (denser cognate web, 800M
  potential readers, Interlingua's 1950s practice evidence) — but *nobody
  has measured it for any pan-Romance conlang*. Cheap, high-value move: run
  an Interslavic-style 7-word cloze test on candidate Romance standards; the
  arXiv 2026 computational metric can pre-screen candidates with zero
  subjects.
- **Community traction.** Interslavic dwarfs the entire Romance field
  combined and demonstrates the repeatable bootstrap loop: merged standard →
  tooling → media moment → legitimacy tokens. In the Romance zone, no
  project owns the niche: Interlingua has the corpus and institutions but a
  frozen standard and aging community; Neolatino has the design energy but
  tiny numbers. A Romance-first strategy is entering an open niche, not
  riding a wave — the wave exists only in Slavic.
- **Orthography for chording.** Best-case regularity is LFN (fully phonemic,
  diacritic-free) but with the least "Romance-standard" legitimacy;
  Interlingua is diacritic-free and ASCII-friendly with mild etymological
  noise (double consonants, Greek digraphs) — mechanically the friendliest
  *naturalistic* Romance target for a chorded layout; Neolatino and
  Interromanico carry accent diacritics and etymological spelling.
  Interslavic is phonemic but dual-script — a chording layer there would
  need Latin/Cyrillic-aware output, which its flavorization culture already
  normalizes.

The Interslavic lesson relevant to input tooling specifically: **receptive
languages bottleneck on writers, so production tooling is a first-class
growth mechanic, not an accessory** — a chorded input method is precisely the
kind of asset (like interslavic.fun's dictionary/flavorizer) that has
historically moved these communities.
